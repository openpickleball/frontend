About OPEN

A pickleball rating anyone can check.

OPEN, the Open Pickleball Evaluation Network, is a free, open-source rating network that no company owns. We are building it in public, and we need players, organizers, and builders to help shape it.

See how you can help Try a sample rating

Early development. No OPEN rating is live yet.

A rating decides which events you can enter, who you get matched with, and how you see your own progress. Something with that much influence should be open to inspection.

  • See it.

    Every rating will come with a clear explanation of how it was calculated.

  • Question it.

    When a number looks wrong, you will be able to challenge it and the method behind it.

  • Take it with you.

    You will be able to export your record and bring it to a different club, league, or app.

OPEN uses the familiar 2.0 to 8.0 scale and works alongside the ratings you already have, so nobody has to choose. Apps, leagues, clubs, and tournaments are partners, and anything they build can use OPEN for free.

01 · How it works

A rating should show its work

OPEN's engine is designed as an Elo variant for doubles and singles. The engine and rating spec aren't published yet, so this sample shows the idea. Drag the final score and follow what happens to each player.

What the engine is designed to account for

Team rating
Combines two partners' ratings to predict a match.
Individual updates
Gives each player their own rating change.
Point differential
Separates a close loss from a decisive one.
Rating uncertainty
Allows larger changes for new players.
Recency
Accounts for skill changing over time.
Match weighting
Can count verified tournament results differently from casual matches.

Every rating will also carry a reliability score from 0 to 100%. It will be earned through matches on OPEN, variety of opponents, and recent play. It can't be chosen.

A 70% favorite should win about 70% of the time.

Illustration only. No validation results exist yet.

Match outcomes are the ground truth. We will judge the engine by how well it predicts matches it hasn't seen, and new ratings will run in shadow mode before anyone relies on them, so early errors cost nobody.

  • Predictive accuracy on future matches only, using Brier score and log loss
  • Stability for established players and quick convergence for new ones
  • Breakdowns by skill level, recreational and tournament play, new players, and separate groups of players
  • Both teams will confirm a score or a verified organizer will submit it. Impossible scores will be rejected, duplicates caught, and outliers flagged.

02 · Promises

Five promises, written into a charter

Everyone who takes part agrees to the mission charter. It can be clarified or strengthened. It can't be weakened.

Free
No subscriptions, paid tiers, advertisements, or premium features, ever.
Transparent
Open-source code and a published, versioned rating spec.
Community-run
No person, company, or sponsor owns or controls it, including the founder.
Portable
Players and leagues can export their data at any time.
One standard
Anyone can read and fork the code, but only the governing board changes the official spec, and only conforming tools can show an OPEN rating.

Players never pay

  • Ratings are free forever.
  • Players are never asked for money, in the app or at events.
  • Donors, grant makers, and sponsors get no say over ratings.
  • Every dollar in and out is published.
  • No advertisements.

Where that stands today

Community control is the goal, and it is not true yet. The five-seat governing board has not been seated, and the founder is the only administrator of the GitHub organization, acting as interim steward.

The steward can't declare a rating official, can't sell or transfer the project to anyone but the board or an entity it controls, and must seat the first board before any OPEN rating goes live. Control of every project asset then passes to the board or a legal entity it controls. Read the governance plan.

03 · Roadmap

What comes next, in rough order

No dates, because the work depends on volunteers.

  1. Now

    • Publish the first versioned rating spec and reference engine
    • Test the engine on real match results in shadow mode

  2. Before launch

    Gate: nothing goes live until these are done

    • Seat the governing board and hand over control
    • Publish validation results
    • Publish a privacy and data policy
    • Define conformance rules and a policy for the OPEN name

  3. Next

    • Results API for platforms, clubs, and tournaments
    • Ratings and stats API, free for any app
    • Organizer tools to upload results, confirm starting ratings, and review flagged matches

  4. Later

    • Public app on web, Android, and iOS with player profiles, clubs, rankings, match history, data export, and a clear explanation of how each rating was calculated

Think something is out of order? Question a priority
